Walking Home

A meditation on Enoch and Elijah

• • •

Pause. Breathe. Let the noise of your mind settle like dust.

“By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death; and was not found, because God had translated him: for before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God.”

— Hebrews 11:5

There is a way of walking with God that has no end.

Not a sprint. Not a religious routine. A walk. Daily. Quiet. So close that the boundary between earth and eternity becomes thin as a veil.

Enoch walked. And one day, he simply walked home. No funeral. No grave. He was not, for God took him.

• • •

What does it mean to “please God”? Not perform. Not prove. But to walk in such union that your presence becomes a pleasing fragrance to Him.

“And it came to pass, as they still went on, and talked, that, behold, there appeared a chariot of fire, and horses of fire, and parted them both asunder; and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.”

— 2 Kings 2:11

Elijah burned. His life was a storm of fire — on Carmel, in the cave, in the wilderness. But his departure came not in battle. It came in the going on, in the talking, in the walking together.

The chariot was not a reward for his fierceness. It was the embrace of a God who would not let His prophet taste death.

• • •

Sweet fellowship of the Spirit — this is the common thread. Enoch walked with God. Elijah walked with God. And the walking itself became the chariot.

“There is a life of intimate communion with God that is not reserved for a few, but is offered to all who will seek it with their whole heart. This is not an achievement; it is a gift received by those who make themselves available to be taken.” — From the desert fathers, adapted

The Door Is Open

These two witnesses stand at the edges of Scripture like burning doorposts. They testify that God is not far off. That intimacy can become so real that death is only a transition, not a terror.

And the same Spirit who walked with Enoch, who sent the chariot for Elijah, is here now. He has not weakened. He has not withdrawn. He waits in the secret place.

Will you walk?

The Upper Room

Honest questions. Gentle answers. A river of intimate communion.

What is this gift?

You may have heard about speaking in tongues. Perhaps it was loud, confusing. But what if praying in tongues is not a performance, but a river of intimacy between the Spirit of God and your spirit?

“For he that speaketh in an unknown tongue speaketh not unto men, but unto God… in the spirit he speaketh mysteries.” — 1 Corinthians 14:2

Paul placed a high value on this gift — not as a badge of superiority, but as a means of private, profound edification: “He that speaketh in an unknown tongue edifieth himself” (1 Cor 14:4). Your spirit, by the Holy Spirit, receiving strength that bypasses your mental limitations.

This is not the mark of a superior Christian. It is a gift of a generous Father. And it is for you.

Why I pray in tongues

I share this because I’ve tasted the sweet fellowship of the Spirit in this way, and I ache for you to taste it too.

Before, my prayer life often felt dry. I would run out of words. I didn’t know how to pray for situations too deep for human language. Then I received the gift. At first, just a syllable. A whisper. But as I yielded, it became a stream. I discovered:

  • 🔥 Worship that transcends English — adoration my mind can’t wrap around.
  • 🔥 Prayer when I have no words — the Spirit prays through me (Romans 8:26).
  • 🔥 Edification in the secret place — my inner man charged from the throne.
  • 🔥 The weight of God’s glory — times His presence became so thick, everything faded.
“And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.” — Acts 2:4

The same Spirit is alive right now. He hasn’t retired. He waits for the willing heart.

Common questions, held gently

Q: Is speaking in tongues for every believer?
The apostle Paul explicitly said, “I would that ye all spake with tongues” (1 Cor 14:5). Peter declared the promise of the Spirit is “unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off” (Acts 2:39). The gift is offered. No one should feel forced or second-class. The Lord is gentle. He leads, He does not drive.
Q: Isn’t this just emotionalism?
Emotion often accompanies deep encounters with God, and that is healthy, not shameful. But tongues is not an emotional crutch. Paul, a man of fierce intellect, practiced it more than anyone (1 Cor 14:18) yet also insisted on order. Emotion may be a companion; it is not the source.
Q: I’m afraid of opening myself to something counterfeit.
This is a holy fear. But Jesus promised: “If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?” (Luke 11:13). Ask the Father, and He will not give you a serpent. The safest place is on your knees, yielding to the Holy Spirit who already dwells in you if you are born again. He will not lead you into deception.
Q: Does the Bible say tongues will cease?
Scripture says that in the perfect, face-to-face presence of God, certain gifts will no longer be needed (1 Corinthians 13:8-10). Until then, we have a Comforter who intercedes for and through us. Until the Bridegroom returns, we need every means of communion He provides.
Q: How do I receive this gift?
With simple, childlike faith. Find a quiet place. Ask the Father to fill you afresh with the Holy Spirit. Thank Him for the promise. Then, as the Spirit prompts, begin to speak — not in English, but in the syllables that rise from your spirit. It may feel like one word, repeated. That is a seed. Water it. It will grow into a river. “Out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water” (John 7:38).
